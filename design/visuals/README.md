# Visuals

*Brio's own imagery, generated at stage 07 step 4. One colour grade for the whole product, and the prompt is recorded here so the style is reproducible rather than remembered. A visual is a system layer: a photograph chosen by feel on each screen would drift the same way an unnamed colour drifts.*

## Why generated rather than stock

Attribute A5 asks for real small teams, documentary, natural light, and explicitly rejects both stock smiles and illustration. Stage 06 used two Unsplash photographs on the concept stand as a stand-in, and they did the job for a stand. They are not the product's own imagery: no control over the grade, no control over what the room contains, and the same picture sits on a thousand other sites. From here the product carries its own.

## The colour grade, and it is one for everything

Cool neutral, low saturation, clean whites and paper grey, a quiet hint of blue in the shadows. No warm orange cast and no teal-orange look. The grade is chosen to sit beside `#F7F8FC` paper and `#346FEC` action without either fighting the other, and it is the same on every image: hero, media slot, program card.

## What a photograph has to pass

- People are working, not celebrating. No high fives, no whiteboard triumph, no laughing at nothing.
- Nobody looks at the camera and nobody poses.
- Daylight, ordinary rooms, ordinary weekday clothes.
- A small team, or one person alone. Not an open plan of a hundred.
- No visible branding, no legible screen content, no logos.
- Real skin texture. Retouched skin reads as an advertisement, and this product is not selling a mood.

## Generation

- **Service:** Magnific. **Model:** Google Nano Banana 2 (`imagen-nano-banana-2-flash`). **Aspect:** 3:2. Two prompts, two variants each, four images generated, two kept.
- **Cost:** 75 credits per image, 300 in total for this set.
- Files are stored at 1600px wide, JPEG quality 82, which is what the pages actually load. The 4K originals stay in Magnific rather than in the repository.

### Prompt 1, `team-table.jpg`

Used by the marketing hero and by the media slot.

> Documentary photograph, not stock: four colleagues in an ordinary small-company office, gathered at a plain wooden table, mid-conversation over printed pages and laptops. Nobody looks at the camera, nobody is laughing or posing, no high fives, no forced smiles. Candid working moment, slightly off-guard, one person mid-sentence. Natural window light from the left, soft and even, no studio lighting, no flare. 35mm lens, waist-up framing, shallow depth of field with the background office falling gently out of focus. Colour grade: cool neutral, low saturation, clean whites and paper-grey walls, a quiet hint of blue in the shadows, no warm orange cast, no teal-orange look. Ordinary weekday clothes, no branding visible, no logos on screens or walls. Real skin texture, no retouching. Editorial reportage feel, calm and unremarkable.

### Prompt 2, `check-in-desk.jpg`

Used where the employee side of the product is shown: one person, alone, answering.

> Documentary photograph, not stock: one person alone at a desk in an ordinary small-company office, answering something on a phone or laptop, seen from a respectful distance. Quiet, private, unhurried moment. No eye contact with the camera, no smile for the lens, no posing. Natural window light, soft, from the side. 35mm lens, three-quarter view, shallow depth of field, plain office background falling out of focus. Colour grade: cool neutral, low saturation, clean whites and paper-grey surfaces, a quiet hint of blue in the shadows, no warm orange cast, no teal-orange look. Everyday clothes, no visible branding or logos, screen content not legible. Real skin texture, no retouching. Calm editorial reportage feel.

## The honest caveat

These are generated people in a generated room. That is a trade: the product gets its own consistent imagery instead of somebody else's stock, and it gives up the claim that these are real Brio customers. Nothing in the copy claims they are, and nothing should: the moment a photograph is captioned as a real team, it becomes a fabricated testimonial. If Brio ever has customers willing to be photographed, their photographs replace these, and this file records exactly what the replacement has to match.
