Field

A label, a control and, when it is needed, a hint or an error. The unit a form is actually made of.

Anatomy

  • .wf-fieldthe stack: label above control, 8px between them
  • .wf-field > .kthe label, at the label step in secondary ink
  • .wf-microthe hint under the control, when the label is not enough
  • .wf-errthe error line, in place of the hint

Variants and sizes

Content

Three, and they are additive: label plus control, plus a hint, or plus an error. A field never carries a hint and an error at the same time, because the error is the thing to read.

The empty cells, and why they are empty

No emphasis axis and no size axis. A field is as wide as the form it is in.

When to use it

Thirty screens. Every place the operator sets something up and every place a person signs in. It is a composition rather than a styled thing: the label belongs to the field, the box belongs to the Input, and neither owns the other.

The rule, and the anti rule

Do this. Always a visible label. A placeholder is a hint, not a label: it disappears the moment a person starts typing, which is exactly when they need to remember what the box was for.

Not this, use Copy field instead. Do not stack two controls in one field. Two answers are two fields, and a value with a button beside it is a Copy field.

States

The states belong to the control inside it and are inherited rather than redeclared. The field itself has nothing to hover.

No snapshots here, and that is the rule rather than an omission. The unit of a snapshot is a difference, not an occurrence. This component answers every state exactly as Input does, so the answer is written in this line instead of proved again with pictures. A matrix with more snapshots than named differences is a list of occurrences, not documentation.
