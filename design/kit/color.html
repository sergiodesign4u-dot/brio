Foundations

Colour

Twenty four primitives and twenty three roles. A primitive says what the value is and where it came from; a role says why the colour is here, what surface it paints, and which threshold it is measured against. A component never reads a primitive colour: it reads a role, and the role is what a theme replaces.

Roles, in both themes at once

Each tile is split: the light half on the left, the dark half on the right. The pair is written in the same breath as the role, because a role without a pair does not exist, and it is deliberately not a mirror. Read the ones marked not a mirror in the tokens file: a recessed surface goes darker than its card in the light theme and lighter in the dark one, and the focus ring takes a lighter step of the blue, not the same blue.

The three rows that sit below their threshold on purpose

--bg-accent-soft measures 1.87 in the light theme and 1.78 in the dark. It never identifies a state on its own, and that is a rule rather than a hope: the selected chip is identified by its action coloured border at 4.53, and the outcome badge by its glyph at 9.02. If a future screen uses this fill as the only sign that something is selected, the fill has to change, not the rule.

--line-hairline at 1.25 separates without enclosing. It draws the edge of a card and the rule between two list rows, and no control depends on it to be found. --bg-skeleton stands in for content that is not there yet.

The two surface separations, card against page at 1.06, are not contrast requirements at all: nothing has to be identified by them, and a card is also drawn by its hairline. They are in the table so that the number is visible rather than assumed.

One role is measured as a glyph and would fail as text

--glyph-action is the shield on the privacy strip, the most repeated glyph in the product, and it measures 4.27 against the page. As a graphical object under WCAG 1.4.11 its threshold is 3:1 and it passes. As text its threshold would be 4.5 and it would fail, which is exactly why this blue is never used for text anywhere in Brio. The number is written down rather than rounded away, because the day someone sets a label in the action colour, this line is the reason not to.
