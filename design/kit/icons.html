Icons

Nine glyphs, one set, one mechanism. Solar linear at stroke 1.5 on a 24 pixel grid, inlined as a data URI and painted through a CSS mask, so a glyph takes the ink of its context instead of carrying a colour of its own. This page is where you take an icon from, instead of searching the screens for one.

The mechanism, and why it is a mask rather than an SVG element

An icon here is a background painted through the shape of the glyph. The element sets color to a glyph role and then background-color: currentColor, and the mask cuts the shape out of it. Three things follow, and each of them is why the mechanism was chosen.

The glyph inherits its ink from a role, so the dark theme moves it without anyone touching a component. It arrives through CSS rather than through a new element, which is what lets a coloured copy add an icon to markup that belongs to the frozen grey original. And because a role paints it, the step 9 grep can prove that no ink role is ever read by a background property: the property here holds currentColor, and the role sits on color where it belongs.

A glyph is measured at 3:1, not 4.5:1

An icon is a graphical object under WCAG 1.4.11, not text. The role that matters is --glyph-action at 4.27 against the page: it passes as a glyph and would fail as text, which is why the action blue is never set as a label anywhere in Brio. The full table is on the colour page.

One size that is not on this page

The drawing grid is 24px and the icon is drawn to it. The 20 and 16 pixel renderings are the same file scaled by the mask, not separate glyphs, which is why a stroke of 1.5 survives all three. A glyph that needed a second drawing at a smaller size would be a second icon and would get its own row.
