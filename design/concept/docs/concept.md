# Concept

*Stage 06 Concept. How Brio found and proved its visual language: designer taste (recorded, not held in the head), visual attributes (each traced to a data line and a borrowed technique), the chosen brand, and the chosen layout. Taste is data too. Every decision downstream must have a pair here or in `references.md`, or it is invention.*

*Companion file: `design/concept/docs/references.md` (Refero sources, base and techniques).*

---

## Base direction (confirmed at Step 1 sign-off)

**Base reference: Signal** (light, privacy-first, human warmth). **Apple** is the primary technique source for restraint (the named "trust ceiling"). **1Password** lends product-as-proof and single-accent discipline. **Culture Amp** and **Oura** lend mechanism content, not look. Full rationale in `references.md`.

Strategic line held throughout: Brio looks like a **privacy and trust product**, not a wellness brand. The category reflex (cream, sage, blobs) is rejected on sight.

---

## Designer's Taste

*Supplied by the designer at Step 2, not invented. Named products, not adjectives. This is the taste filter every attribute and every later decision must pass.*

### Likes (named products, and the specific visual thing taken)

- **Signal** - privacy made *visible* in the UI, with human warmth and no infantilism. The specific thing: light airy split sections (one calm sentence beside one product visual), one calm blue as the only accent on flat backgrounds, product screenshots as the proof. This is also the base.
- **Linear** - precision and engineered restraint. The specific thing: dense, confident typography and disciplined spacing carry the character, near-monochrome, almost no decoration. "Quiet competence" comes from type and rhythm, not ornament.
- **monobank** - character without corporateness. The specific thing: a trustworthy product can still have personality; the life is in the small details and copy, never in playful blobs. Character and seriousness are not opposites.

### Anti-references (what we will not do)

- **Cream + sage "wellness" palette, blob illustration.** The category reflex; it makes Brio read as "just another wellness app" and competes on the axis Brio loses. (Rejected in the Refero search: August Health, Function Health, Ease Health, Headspace.)
- **Playful blobs, big illustrations, emoji, motivational tone.** Directly contradicts `voice/docs/voice.md` P5 (no emoji, no exclamations, no motivational tone) and P3 (honest, never flattering).
- **Corporate stock-SaaS.** Smiling stock people, walls of dashboards and charts, faceless enterprise polish. Contradicts the primary persona: Priya is a non-expert who needs plain competence, not enterprise theater (`personas.md` O3).

**Note:** the designer did *not* reject a dark, cinematic register. So a considered dark accent or a dark mode is allowed later, but the base stays light (Signal). Darkness, if used, must read as calm and precise (Linear), never as luxury drama.

## Attributes

*3 to 5 pairs of visual opposites. Each pair is built from a specific data line and a specific borrowed technique. No pair contradicts Designer's Taste. A color, shape, or type choice with no pair here or in `references.md` is invention: name the source or drop it.*

### A1. Calm and plain, not clinical or surveillance-flavored

- **From the data:** benchmark **C6** (team reporting must feel honest and non-judgmental, "rather than clinical, corporate, or surveillance-flavored"); Yemi **E3/E4** (the first check-in is the trust-forming moment and must feel safe); `voice.md` **P5** (quiet against a loud category).
- **Technique (`references.md`):** Signal's light split sections and one soft blue on flat backgrounds. No dark data-terminal, no clinical grid. The surface itself should lower the guard, not raise it.
- **Amended at stage 07 step 6, decided from the mockups:** the word was "airy", and the product is deliberately not airy. Layout direction 02, the working desk, puts the pulse, the participation, the privacy line and the next program on one screen without scrolling, because that is what the operator needs weekly (D-15). The attribute now reads **light and unhurried at any density**: paper against white, hairlines instead of shadows, one accent, generous line height. Density is allowed; noise is not, and every row added to the rail still has to earn its place.

### A2. Proof shown, not privacy promised

- **From the data:** CLAUDE.md and benchmark **Mechanism 2** (privacy made visible in the product UI, not promised in a policy); Marcus **W3** (trusts architecture over marketing copy); Yemi **E5** (a specific sentence, shown, not a policy link).
- **Technique (`references.md`):** 1Password and Signal product-screenshot-as-proof. "What the owner can and cannot see" is rendered as the *actual owner view*, framed as evidence, never as a paragraph of reassurance.

### A3. Restrained and exact, not loud or persuasive

- **From the data:** `voice.md` **P5** (no buzzwords, superlatives, or exclamations) and **P2** (privacy as one specific mechanism sentence); Marcus **W3** ("privacy language that sounds like marketing copy" scares him off).
- **Technique (`references.md`):** Apple restraint (claims as calm editorial fact, depth from tonal and surface contrast rather than shadows, one accent as the single interactive color) plus Linear's dense, confident type. The look earns trust by looking structural, not sold.

### A4. Interpretation carried, not a number left bare

- **From the data:** Priya **O6** ("a score without context produces no action"; the aha is score + interpretation + next action) and **O3** (she cannot read 67% as good or bad alone); `voice.md` **P1** (a number never appears without its interpretation) and **P4** (always a next step).
- **Technique (`references.md`):** Signal's "one sentence beside one visual" rhythm applied to the pulse card (the Stage 04 etalon on Dashboard): the read and the next action sit visually *with* the score, never as a lone metric tile.

### A5. Personable and human, not faceless-corporate nor infantile

- **From the data:** Designer's Taste (Signal warmth, monobank character); `research/docs/ux-patterns.md` **B4** ("a knowledgeable peer, not a data terminal"); both anti-references rejected at once (corporate stock-SaaS *and* playful blobs).
- **Technique (`references.md`):** Signal's human warmth plus monobank's character-in-the-details, rendered with photography of real working teams (not stock-smiley, not blob illustration) and Linear's disciplined type. The tone is a colleague who has done this before, calm and specific.
- **Amended at stage 07 step 6, decided from the mockups:** photography carries A5 **where the product is being explained**, which is the public surfaces and the sign-up panel, and where a wireframe actually holds a media slot. Inside the operator dashboard and the employee check-in there is no photograph and there should not be one: a picture of strangers on the screen where somebody reports a hard week is decoration, and decoration is what A3 rules out. In the interior the human register is carried by plain wording, by the human scale of the type, and by the soft accent. From stage 07 the imagery is Brio's own, generated in one recorded colour grade, rather than stock (`design/visuals/README.md`).

## Brand

*Filled at Step 3b. Chosen 18 August 2026.*

**Chosen: `assets/brand-plate-a.png`.** Decision of the user. Plate A is taken as the base: a light airy surface, one blue accent used only for action, soft cards, and real photography of people. It answers A1 (calm and plain, a surface that lowers the guard rather than a dark data terminal), A3 (one accent as the single interactive colour) and A5 (human rather than faceless), and it sits closest to the base direction Signal recorded at Step 1.

Two things on the plate are raw material and do not ship, because they argue with rules already recorded: the headline "Your privacy is our priority" is a promise where A2 and `voice.md` P2 require the mechanism to be shown, and the stock meeting photograph is exactly the stock smile A5 rejects. Both are named in `DESIGN-artifacts.md` under "Corrections applied to plate A", together with the third and larger risk: a cornflower blue accent sits near the category reflex that `docs/decisions.md` D-13 warns about, and is accepted knowingly, with the differentiation carried by the mechanism zones and the restraint rather than by hue.

**Recorded and not chosen,** so we can return to any of them:

| Plate | Character | Why not chosen |
|---|---|---|
| B | Indigo, uppercase, "engineered" | Enterprise theatre, against A1 and A5 |
| C | Near black with warm amber | Amber on the primary action reads as a warning; mockup text degraded |
| D | Light paper with teal | Strongest rival: carried the owner CAN and CANNOT panel, the pre-check-in disclosure and the minimum-of-5 poster |
| E | Graphite with ice blue | Dark base, while the base is fixed as light. Available later as a dark register |
| F | Light paper with plum and moss | Plum reads editorial and wine-like; moss as the positive colour drifts back to the wellness palette |

The values taken from plate A live in the root `DESIGN-artifacts.md`, sampled from pixels rather than from the captions printed on the image.

## Layout

*Filled at Step 5. Chosen 18 August 2026.*

**Chosen: direction 02, "Working desk, two columns"** (`directions.html`). The pulse, the participation, the privacy line and the next program sit on one screen without scrolling, which is the daily reality of the primary persona and the two minute owner review. A4 survives the density, because the read and the next step stay inside the pulse card and never split into a bare metric tile.

The named risk, carried forward rather than forgotten: density is the shortest path to the clinical, surveillance-flavoured feel that A1 rules out. Every row added to the rail has to earn its place, and that is a standing constraint on stage 07.

**Recorded and not chosen:**

| Direction | Character | Why not chosen |
|---|---|---|
| 01 Single column, wide air | One narrow column, large photograph, one thing at a time | The calmest of the three, but it pushes participation and the privacy line below the fold on the screen the operator uses weekly. It stays the right shape for the employee check-in, where there is one question and one action |
| 03 Evidence band | Full width photograph, permanent owner CAN and CANNOT panel | The strongest expression of A2, but a permanent proof panel competes with the one main action per screen, and the band costs too much height at 360px. The panel itself stays in the language as a pattern on the privacy surfaces |

## Divergence between the attributes and the mockups, decided

*Written at stage 07 step 1, decided at step 6. From here on the mockups are the living truth and this file follows them, but an attribute is not overwritten silently: it was derived at stage 06 from a line in `personas.md`, and it is what keeps the look tied to data about people. Both rows below were resolved by amending the attribute rather than the mockup, and both amendments are written into the attributes above so nobody has to read this table to know the rule.*

| Attribute | The line it grew from | What the mockups actually do | The two ways out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A5** Personable and human, technique: "real photography of real small teams" | Designer's taste, and `ux-patterns.md` B4, "a knowledgeable peer, not a data terminal" | Neither coloured product screen carries a photograph. 4.0 Dashboard and the whole check-in have no media placeholder in the grey original, and a copy owns the visual layer only, so there is nowhere to put one. Warmth on these screens comes from the wording, the soft accent and the human scale of the type | **Fix the mockup:** open the IA and the wireframe, add a media slot to a product screen, then colour it. **Amend the attribute:** say that photography carries A5 on the public and marketing surfaces and on the stand, while inside the product the human register is carried by plain wording and by the soft accent, never by stock imagery |
| **A1** Calm and plain, technique: "Signal's light, airy split sections" | Benchmark C6, reporting must feel honest and non-judgmental rather than clinical; Yemi E3 and E4 | The operator screen is deliberately dense: layout direction 02, the working desk, chosen at stage 06 step 5 so the pulse, the participation, the privacy line and the next program sit on one screen without scrolling. It is light, but it is not airy | **Fix the mockup:** give the dashboard back its air and accept that participation and the privacy line fall below the fold on the screen used weekly. **Amend the attribute:** keep "calm and plain" and replace "airy" with "light and unhurried at any density", recording that D-15 already accepted the density together with the constraint that every row added to the rail has to earn its place |

**Decision on both rows: the attribute was amended, not the mockup.** In each case the mockup turned out to be the more honest reading of the same persona line. A5 grew out of "a knowledgeable peer, not a data terminal", and a peer does not hang photographs of strangers in the room where you report a hard week; it speaks plainly. A1 grew out of "honest and non-judgmental rather than clinical", and what makes a screen non-clinical is tone and hierarchy, not empty space. Neither amendment touches what the attribute claims, only the technique line that said how it would be carried. The reasons stay here because after an attribute is aligned to a mockup, nobody would ever see the difference again.
